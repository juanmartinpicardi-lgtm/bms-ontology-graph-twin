# Assessment – Will the rules survive a real user? Where AI is needed, and where it isn't

Status: 2026-11-23. Evidence: `scripts/stress_intake.py`, results in `reports/stress_intake.json`. Feeds
`plan-workflow-ai.md` (W0 and W6 changed as a result).

## 1. The question

A real user uploads a new building: another engineering firm's drawings, another controls vendor's submittal,
another BMS's export, maybe a scanned set. Does the guided setup, which is rules only today, still build a usable
graph? If not, where exactly does it need a model, and where would a model be the wrong fix?

## 2. Verdict

**No, not as it stands.** The readers are accurate on the one building they were written for, and they break on
ordinary variations from other firms. When they break, most of the time they don't say so.

**Yes, we need more AI, in one place: reading documents.** That means finding the structure of a page (which sheet
is a plan, where a schedule's columns are, where a sequence's sections start, what each column of an export means)
and reading drawings. It doesn't mean more AI in the middle of the pipeline. Ontology mapping, cross-checks, feeds
rules, validation and scoring should stay deterministic. There, rules aren't the weakness; they're what makes a model's
answer checkable.

**Two failures aren't about AI at all, and come first:**
- **Silent failure.** A reader that finds nothing must say so, and hand over to a model or a person.
- **No second test set.** Every number we have was measured on the building the rules were written for.

## 3. Why the current numbers flatter us

- Every rule in `intake/` was written against one document set. Troop D is one state bid set from one engineering
  firm, as vector PDFs.
- The submittal and BMS export are generated by our own code (`troop_d.py`), in the format our reader expects.
  Measuring the submittal reader on them is circular.
- `tests/test_intake.py` asserts perfect scores on that same package. It protects against regressions. It says
  nothing about a new building.
- There is no held-out document set. `spec-ai-architecture.md` §7 already names the need (Kelly Walsh). It's still
  open.

## 4. What breaks: the stress test

Each scenario changes one thing the way another firm, vendor or user realistically would. Everything else stays the
real Troop D package. The changes are applied to the words read from the PDF, or to the CSV rows, so nothing on disk
changes.

"Asks" is the number of questions the reader puts to the user. Zero asks with zero results means a **silent
failure**.

  free-text descriptions, in-distribution. The `point_class` model job exists, but only the BMS mapper uses it; the
  guided setup doesn't.

### 4.1 After the first guardrails (W0, 23 Nov 2026)

`intake/coverage.py` now runs after every step, and a few rules were widened:
- FLR titles
- Niagara export columns
- the device instance join
- 4-digit rooms with a wing digit
- point rows the submittal couldn't read

`tests/test_stress_intake.py` runs all 16 variations. Each is classed by `coverage.stress_state`:

| Outcome | Count | Scenarios |
|---|---|---|
| Reads correctly (within 10% of the original) | 9 | FLR titles, 4-digit rooms, room number above its name, Niagara columns, devices by instance, AIR TERMINAL titles, the 3 whole-set uploads |
| Short, and says so and asks | 5 | Scanned plans, Title Case sequences, BUS-n trunks, spelled-out object types, Niagara references |

| Silent (zero and no question) | 0 | |

**Partial-read checks, the same day.** The two quiet cases were fixed:
- Schedules now count rows in a tag column that the tag rules don't know.
- The submittal says when a typical point list matches no controller, or when more than 25% of controllers match no
  unit.
- The BMS step says when more than 25% of its devices are unlinked.
- Sequences flag system headings whose sections can't be read.
- Numbered sections (1. 2. 3.) are now read like lettered ones.

A new variation, sections as bullets, keeps the sequence check under test. The result over 17 variations:

| Outcome | Count |
|---|---|
| Reads correctly | 10 |
| Short, says so and asks | 7 |
| Quiet | 0 |
| Silent | 0 |

`tests/test_stress_intake.py` now fails on any new quiet or silent case.

The answer key uses Troop D's own names. So in variations that rename things (room 1109, device DEV3104), the
scorer shows 0 even when everything was read; the stress script records raw counts for that reason. The 2 quiet
scenarios are the next guardrails, and they are allowed in the test only as a known list that may shrink, never grow.
The 5 that ask are where the AI reader (W6, W8) starts.

**What the stress test doesn't cover:** layout changes that need a real PDF from another firm. Examples: schedule
columns in another order (the column layouts in `schedules.py` are positional, so a moved column shifts values
silently), multi-page tables, rotated sheets, plans with zones drawn as shaded areas, and new document types
(Excel point lists, IFC/Revit, O&M manuals, TAB reports). Expect those to be at least as bad.

## 5. Why: what each reader assumes

| Reader | Hard-coded assumption (file) | Real-world variety |
|---|---|---|
| Floor plans | Floor only from titles like `PLAN - LEVEL n`, `FIRST FLOOR … PLAN`, `ROOF PLAN`; rooms are `^\d{3}[A-Z]?$` with the name above the number; a text layer exists (`floorplans.py`) | Title blocks vary by firm; rooms 3 or 4 digits, alphanumeric, or names only; many retrofit sets are scanned |
| Schedules | A title containing "SCHEDULE" at 8 pt or more; a layout chosen by title words (`FAN POWERED`, `VAV TERMINAL`…) with **columns by position** (`schedules.py`) | Each firm names and orders columns differently, units differ (L/s, kW), tables span pages |
| Sequences | System headings are ALL CAPS ending in a colon; sections are `A.`, `B.` (`sequence_text.py`) | CSI spec sections (3.1 A. 1. a.), Title Case, Guideline 36 references, prose without headings |
| Submittal | Riser under `FC-n - …` headings, rows `addr device controller instance location`; point rows end in `AI/AO/BI/BO…` and a terminal (`UI1`); room rows `L1 109 …`; Metasys references (`submittal.py`) | Every controls vendor (JCI, Siemens, Honeywell, Distech/Niagara, ALC, Trane) has its own submittal format |
| BMS export | Columns from a fixed synonym list; device linked to equipment only through tags in the device name (`bms.py`) | Each BMS exports its own columns; device names are often instance numbers or controller models |
| Tags | 15 prefixes in `equipment_tags.yaml`, prefix plus number | Floor-embedded tags, numbering by zone, owner standards |

Every one of these can be patched with another rule, and each patch covers one more firm. That's the trap: an
endless list of formats, each found only when a user's upload fails.

## 6. Where AI earns its place, and where it doesn't

### 6.1 Needed: a document-understanding front end

Move models to the front of every PDF reader. Turn today's parsers into **checkers**.

```
page ──► model: page type + structure as JSON ──► rules: normalise, check, cross-check ──► facts (proposed)
           (sheet type, floor, rooms with ids,        (tags, units, classes from the
            table header → field map, riser rows,      knowledge model, room vs plan,
            sequence outline, column meanings)         schedule vs plan, totals)
```

- The model reads **structure**, which varies by firm: which sheet is a floor plan and which floor, where a table's
  header is and what each column means, where a sequence's sections start, what a riser row's fields are.
- The rules do what they're good at, and what a model mustn't be trusted with alone: tag normalisation, Brick
  classes from the knowledge model, unit conversion, every cross-document check, and the answer key.
- Model output is checked like any source. A room the model reports must have a label word on the page. A schedule
  row must start with a tag the plans know, or it's asked about. A point must have a reference the BMS confirms. What
  doesn't check out is a question, not a fact.
- **Once per firm or vendor, a structure becomes a rule.** The model's header map for a firm's VAV schedule, or for a
  vendor's point schedule, is saved as a layout skill (`spec-memory-skills.md`). From then on, that firm's next
  building reads with rules alone. The cost per building falls, and the rules grow from real uploads instead of
  guesses.

Per stage:

| Stage | AI job | Why rules can't | Model (`ai/models.py`) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Page sorting (new) | Classify every page of any upload: plan, schedule, sequence, riser, point schedule, room schedule, detail, spec, other | Users drop whole sets and unknown document types | Gemini 4 Hydrogen (cheap, reads PDFs natively) |
| Floor plans | Floor name, room labels (number and name, any style), tags with their rooms; rooms from the image when there is no text layer | Title blocks, label styles and scans vary without limit | Hydrogen; Sonnet 5.5 or Opus 5.5 for hard sheets |
| Schedules | Table header → field map, multi-page tables, units | Column names and order are a firm's habit | Opus 5.5 once per firm (layout skill); Haiku 5.5 to check rows |
| Sequences | Outline (systems, sections), then loops, interlocks and setpoints as structured logic | Prose; every spec style differs | Sonnet 5.5 |
| Submittal | Riser rows and point schedules from any vendor's layout | Every vendor's format differs | Hydrogen; Sonnet 5.5 escalate |
| Point classes | Description → Brick class, from candidates | 51% exact by rules even in-distribution | Haiku 5.5 (`point_class`, already built: wire it into the setup) |
| BMS export | Column meanings for an unknown export | Every BMS exports differently | Haiku 5.5, once per file |

### 6.2 Not needed: keep these as rules

- **Ontology mapping and validation.** Classes come from the knowledge model. SHACL decides validity. A model never
  invents a class.
- **Cross-document checks.** Schedule vs plans, submittal rooms vs plans, BMS vs submittal. These are what catch a
  model's mistakes.
- **Feeds and part-of rules, tag normalisation, unit conversion.** Exact and cheap.
- **Some of the failures above are rule gaps, not AI gaps:**
  - Devices named by instance: the riser already lists each controller's device instance, so join on it.
  - BMS column synonyms such as `Path`, `Parent` and `Display Name` should be added.
  - The room-number pattern should accept 4 digits and letters.

  Fix these deterministically, and still put the model in front for the next format nobody has seen.

### 6.3 Needed regardless of AI: guardrails

1. **Coverage checks with escalation.** Each reader states what it expected and what it found. Examples: a sheet
   titled like a plan with 0 rooms; a schedule title with 0 rows; a sequence page with 0 headings; an export with no
   device column. When coverage is low, the page goes to the model (or a person), and the user sees it.
   **No stage may end with zero results and zero questions.**
2. **A robustness suite in the tests.** `scripts/stress_intake.py` becomes a test. Every scenario must either read
   correctly or ask. Silent zeros fail the build.
3. **More real document sets.** At least 3 more, from different engineering firms and controls vendors, with one set
   held out as a test set and never tuned on (`CLAUDE.md` §5). Without them, no accuracy claim, rules or model, means
   anything.

## 7. Cost, risk and what it takes

**Rough cost per building.** These are assumed token counts at Nov 2026 Vertex prices, not measured.

| Item | Assumption | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Page sorting | 30 to 50 pages | about $0.20 |
| Plan sheets | ~10k tokens in, 3k out per sheet, on Hydrogen | about $0.04 a sheet |
| Escalations | 20% of pages to Opus 5.5 | about $0.10 a page |
| Points | 1,000 to 5,000 points on Haiku 5.5 | under $1 |

That's likely **under $10 to $20 per building**, against days of engineering time. Cost isn't the constraint;
accuracy and verification are.

**Risks and mitigations:**
- **Invented rooms or points.** The model answers in ids and words found on the page. Cross-checks and calibrated
  confidence catch the rest, and nothing is confirmed without a second source or a person.
- **Different answers on re-read.** Cache results by file hash and prompt version; re-read only when either changes.
- **Customer data.** Vertex AI on our GCP project only (open question in `plan-workflow-ai.md` §8).
- **Latency.** Page reading runs in parallel; batch for re-reads. A 30-sheet set should take minutes, not hours.

## 8. Recommendation

1. **Now (no AI):**
   - Coverage checks and escalation in every reader.
   - The stress suite as a test.
   - The deterministic fixes in §6.2.
   - Wire `point_class` into the guided setup.
   - Start collecting 3 more real document sets.
2. **Next: the document-understanding front end.** Start with page sorting and floor plans, the stage that fails
   worst and that users see first. Then schedules (layout skills per firm), then submittals and sequences. Each
   passes the bake-off in `plan-workflow-ai.md` §6.4 on the new real sets, not on Troop D alone.
3. **Keep the middle deterministic.** Ontology, checks, validation and scoring stay rules. They are what let us
   trust the models at the front.
